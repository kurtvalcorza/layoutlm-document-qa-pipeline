#### Description

`impira/layoutlm-document-qa` is Impira's fine-tune of Microsoft's LayoutLM (v1) for extractive question answering over documents — "LayoutLM: Pre-training of Text and Layout for Document Image Understanding" (Xu et al., arXiv:1912.13318) fine-tuned, per the snapshot README, on SQuAD 2.0 and DocVQA — pinned here to revision `beed3c4d02d86017ebca5bd0fdf210046b907aa6` (the Hub's `main` on 2026-09-14). The snapshot `config.json` declares `LayoutLMForQuestionAnswering`: a 12-layer BERT-style encoder (hidden size 768, 12 heads, intermediate width 3072) whose input embeddings add 2-D position embeddings for each token's box on a 0–1000 grid (`max_2d_position_embeddings` 1024) to a RoBERTa-style token embedding (vocabulary 50,265, `max_position_embeddings` 514, RoBERTa byte-level BPE tokenizer with `<s>`/`</s>` specials) and a span head predicting start and end logits — 127,792,898 parameters in the 511 MB float32 `model.safetensors`. **The model reads words and boxes, not pixels**: the caller supplies the page's OCR tokens with their pixel boxes, the pipeline normalises the boxes to the grid, encodes `<s> question </s></s> words </s>` (question tokens and specials at box `[0,0,0,0]`, separators at `[1000]*4`, word tokens at their boxes — the transformers document-question-answering pipeline's convention), splits documents longer than 512 tokens into overlapping windows (stride 128), and selects the span of at most 15 tokens whose start×end softmax product is highest across windows, returning it as a word span with indices. No adaptation happens at inference. What this repository adds is packaging and a bounded adaptation contract: `verify_snapshot` and `stage_missing_files` (manifest digest checking and fresh-clone staging), `LayoutLMDocumentQAPipeline.from_pretrained` (verified local loading with `trust_remote_code=False`), `answer` (input validation, box normalisation, windowing, span decoding), `normalize_box`, the optional `ocr_words_with_tesseract` adapter (imports `pytesseract` only when called; not part of the pinned runtime), the `normalize_answer` / `anls` / `exact_match` helpers with the `validate_inputs` and `evaluation_report` stage helpers, corpus-level metrics with two non-neural baselines (`metrics.py`: last number and keyword lookup), a digest-pinned real gold-span corpus read one column at a time (`samples.py`: CORD-v2 receipts turned into templated questions, validated and split by page without leakage), a fit check that drops rather than truncates the records that would need more than one window, supervised fine-tuning of the last *k* encoder blocks and the span head (`adapt`, default 4 of 12) with validation-ANLS epoch selection, and a safetensors adapter (`save_artifact` / `from_artifact`) that carries only the trained tensors and is bound to the base weight digest.

#### Intended Use and Limitations

The uses below are the ones the package was built to support; everything else is either out of scope (§Out-of-scope use cases) or prohibited (§Use cases).

###### Primary Intended Uses

The task is extractive document question answering over OCR output: input one question (up to 256 characters), the page's words (1–2000) with one pixel `[x0, y0, x1, y1]` box per word and the page size; output the best answer span as text, its inclusive start/end word indices (so the answer's boxes are known), the span score and the number of windows used. Envisioned applications are field extraction from forms, invoices, receipts, letters and reports that already pass through an OCR engine — the DocQuery use case Impira built the model for — where the fields can be phrased as questions and the returned indices localise the answer on the page for review; triage of scanned business documents; and interactive lookup over an OCR'd page. The second intended use is bounded domain adaptation: given 8–20,000 `{id, page_id, question, words, boxes, image_size, answer_start, answer_end}` records with gold spans over the operator's own OCR output, `adapt` fine-tunes the last four encoder blocks and the span head (28,353,026 parameters, about 22 % of the model) for a few epochs, selects the epoch by validation ANLS, and `save_artifact` exports the trained tensors as a ~113 MB safetensors adapter that `from_artifact` applies on top of the digest-verified base — the recorded run lifts held-out CORD-v2 ANLS from 0.843 to 0.942 in about sixteen minutes of CPU training, concentrated in the fields the frozen model missed. The pipeline is an inference component and a zero-configuration baseline for OCR-based document QA plus a recipe for adapting it to one document family, not a certified extractor for any specific document family, and it ships no OCR.

###### Primary Intended Users

Intended users are machine-learning engineers, document-processing developers, and data analysts integrating document question answering into research prototypes or in-house document tooling, who already have an OCR stage (Tesseract, a cloud OCR, a PDF text layer with positions), and who may hold a small set of pages annotated with word boxes and gold spans for adaptation. A user is expected to understand that the answer can only ever be a contiguous span of the words they supplied — a mis-read, split or mis-ordered OCR word propagates unchanged — that the span score is the product of two within-window softmaxes under the model's own head, a ranking signal that is not calibrated and says nothing about whether the page can answer the question (the model always returns its best span), that boxes must be in the page's pixel frame and are coarsened to a 1000-step grid, that pages longer than 512 tokens are answered window by window, that the fine-tuning data is English business documents so other languages, handwriting and unusual layouts are distribution shifts, that ANLS and exact match measure agreement with gold spans rather than usefulness and are only comparable across runs scored on the same gold with the same normalisation and the same OCR, and that a fine-tuned adapter is only as good as the split discipline behind it — every question on a page must stay in one split, and pages from few sources must be split by source, or the validation score is inflated. Users who need OCR, abstention, multi-page reasoning, computed answers or batch throughput are expected to know none of that is provided here.

###### Out-of-scope use cases

1. **Capability boundary:** no OCR (words and boxes are inputs; the Tesseract adapter is optional and unpinned), no abstention or "no answer" output (the model returns its best span for every question), no answers that are not a contiguous span of the supplied words, no arithmetic, comparison or multi-hop reasoning, no multi-page or PDF handling (one page per call), no batching, and no confidence beyond the uncalibrated span score. Not a general training framework: `adapt` fine-tunes only whole encoder blocks (1–12 of them, the last *k*) plus the span head, with AdamW, a fixed learning rate, no scheduler, no mixed precision, no gradient accumulation and no early stopping beyond epoch selection; the word, position and 2-D box embeddings and the vocabulary are never modified, so it cannot teach the model a new script, and it trains on pages that fit one window only.
2. **Input boundary:** `answer` rejects non-string or empty questions and questions longer than `MAX_QUESTION_CHARS = 256`, more than `MAX_WORDS = 2000` words or empty words, box lists that do not match the words, boxes that are not four numbers, boxes outside the page or inverted, and page sizes outside `MIN_IMAGE_SIDE = 1`..`MAX_IMAGE_SIDE = 10000` px or not integer pairs (`TypeError`/`ValueError`). Documents beyond 512 tokens are windowed with a 128-token stride, so an answer that straddles a window boundary without falling wholly inside either window is missed; spans are capped at `MAX_ANSWER_TOKENS = 15` tokens, so a long free-text answer is cut. Datasets for `validate_dataset`, `evaluate` and `adapt` are lists of `{id, page_id, question, words, boxes, image_size, answer_start, answer_end}` records: fewer than `MIN_RECORDS = 8` or more than `MAX_RECORDS = 20000` records, a duplicate or malformed `id` (pattern `^[A-Za-z0-9_.:-]{1,64}$`), any page or question the inference checks would refuse, a span outside the words, non-integer span indices, or an `answers` list whose first entry is not the span text is rejected with `ValueError`; `evaluate` accepts 1–`MAX_EVAL_RECORDS = 2000` records; `adapt` requires at least `MIN_RECORDS` training records, refuses any training record that needs more than one window (use `check_fit` first — nothing is truncated), and refuses `epochs` outside 1..20, `batch_size` outside 1..64, `lr` outside (0, 1e-3] or `trainable_encoder_layers` outside 1..12. `load_artifact` refuses an adapter whose `format`, `format_version`, `base_model` id, revision or `weight_sha256` differ from the constants, whose tensor set differs from what its recorded `trainable_encoder_layers` implies, whose tensor names are not `layoutlm.encoder.layer.*` or `qa_outputs.*`, or whose file digest differs from its manifest.
3. **Input boundary:** the fine-tuning data is SQuAD 2.0 (English Wikipedia text) and DocVQA (scanned English business documents from the UCSF Industry Documents Library with short-span answers) — and the OCR conventions of DocVQA's provided tokens. Pages in other languages and scripts, handwriting, OCR that tokenises differently (sub-word fragments, merged cells), reading orders that differ from the OCR's, and questions whose answer is not a span on the page fall outside what the upstream authors report and what this repository measured; results on them are undefined, not merely degraded. The tutorial's rendered invoice has renderer-supplied OCR and the CORD-v2 sample has annotated words and boxes; neither is evidence about any real OCR engine.
4. **Decision boundary:** not for autonomous decisions that act on extracted values — automated invoice payment, claims adjudication, KYC or identity checks, clinical or legal record extraction — without a human comparing the answer span (its boxes are returned for exactly this purpose) with the page, and a locally measured ANLS on the deployment's own labelled pages with the deployment's own OCR. An ANLS gain on the operator's validation split is not a release decision: it is a single seeded split of one corpus with no dispersion estimate.

#### Factors

###### Groups

This pipeline is not human-centric by design: it selects a span of OCR words that answers a question and never classifies, identifies or scores people. The fine-tuning data (SQuAD 2.0's Wikipedia paragraphs; DocVQA's 12,767 scanned industry documents with 50,000 questions, per the dataset paper) contains no evaluation groups in the demographic sense, and neither Impira nor this repository audited it for anything of the kind; the adaptation sample (CORD-v2, 1,000 photographed Indonesian receipts, of which this repository reads 200 annotations) adds one vendor population — Indonesian shops and restaurants — and no demographic labels. What does vary is the document population: DocVQA is English-language, typed or printed, mid-twentieth-century-onward industry correspondence and forms, and CORD is Indonesian receipts with Latin-script item names and rupiah amounts, so pages in other languages and scripts, handwritten or historical typesetting, modern designer layouts and non-Western document conventions are the groups whose answer accuracy is unknown, not known to be equal — compounded by whatever OCR the operator uses, whose own error rates vary by script and print quality. Where pages carry personal data — medical records, HR files, identity documents, correspondence naming individuals — a question turns the pipeline into a targeted extractor; the operator who processes such documents is responsible for a fairness and privacy audit on their own page set, stratified by document family, before relying on the output or adapting to it.

###### Instrumentation

The upstream fine-tuning "instrument" is the OCR that produced DocVQA's word tokens and boxes over archival scans, plus SQuAD's clean text with no layout at all; the model learned the token boundaries, reading order and box conventions of that OCR. Inference inputs arrive from whatever OCR the operator runs — Tesseract at some page-segmentation mode, a cloud OCR, a PDF text layer — and each tokenises, orders and boxes words differently; the pipeline coarsens boxes to a 1000-step grid and validates only that they are numbers inside the page. It cannot detect a mis-read word, a wrong reading order, a merged or split token, or a box on the wrong line, and every such error passes through to the answer and, in adaptation, into the gold spans. The adaptation sample's instrument is CORD's human annotation — quadrilaterals per printed word, in line order, with a field category and an `is_key` flag per word — which this repository flattens to axis-aligned boxes clipped to the page: cleaner than any OCR engine on a photographed receipt, so the recorded gain is an upper bound on what the same recipe yields over real OCR. The rendered invoice is the opposite extreme: its words and boxes come from the renderer (`ImageDraw.textbbox`), an instrument that is exact by construction and that no OCR reproduces.

Operating environment: Python 3.12 with `torch==2.14.0`, `torchvision==0.29.0`, `torchaudio==2.11.0`, `transformers==4.57.6`, `safetensors==0.8.0`, `numpy==2.5.3`, `pillow==11.3.0`, `huggingface-hub==0.36.2`, `pyarrow==25.0.1`, float32 on CPU; CUDA is used automatically when visible (float32). No OCR engine is installed: `pytesseract` is not a dependency and no run calls the adapter. Inference, measured on the reference machine with the GPU hidden (`CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=-1`) and the Hub offline (`HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1`): `verify_snapshot` on the 8-file, 514 MB snapshot 0.27 s; load 5.10 s; five questions over a 73-word page 0.29 s (first call) then 0.11–0.12 s each; an unanswerable question 0.12 s; a 657-word page (the form's words repeated nine times) answered across 4 windows in 0.47 s. Adaptation on the same machine, from the committed notebook's recorded run (Intel Core Ultra 9 275HX, CPU float32): reading the two cached CORD-v2 columns and cutting 595 / 152 / 229 records by receipt took under two seconds; the fit check dropped nothing; scoring the frozen model on the 229 test questions took 41.0 s (ANLS 0.843, exact match 0.782; last-number baseline 0.406 / 0.253; keyword-lookup baseline 0.635 / 0.594); six epochs of AdamW over 595 training questions at batch 16 with the last four encoder blocks and the span head trainable (28,353,026 of 127,792,898 parameters, `lr=3e-5`, weight decay 0.01, gradient clipping 1.0, seed 0) took 934.3 s including the per-epoch validation scoring (validation ANLS 0.819 frozen → 0.870 → 0.908 → 0.894 → 0.925 → 0.960 → 0.956; epoch 5 selected); re-scoring the test split gave ANLS 0.942 and exact match 0.930; the adapter is 113,419,976 bytes over 66 tensors, and the reloaded pipeline reproduced 8/8 answers. Counter-examples recorded during the build on the same split on the RTX 5070 Ti (CUDA, float32): two trainable blocks reached test ANLS 0.854 after three epochs at `lr=3e-5` and 0.909 after six epochs at `lr=5e-5`; three blocks at `lr=5e-5` reached 0.926; four blocks at `lr=5e-5` reached 0.936 against 0.946 at `lr=3e-5` — the default is the four-block, `lr=3e-5`, six-epoch configuration. The CUDA path was executed by the model-backed test suite on that GPU (answer, a one-epoch adaptation and the artifact round trip) and by those probes; the Hub-download path was executed by the earlier Kaggle run of the inference-only notebook. Data environment: the model assumes English or Latin-script, machine-readable OCR of a business document in reading order with one box per word, and a question answerable by a short span; the rendered form and the CORD annotations satisfy that assumption exactly and are where the measured behaviour holds. Real OCR noise, other languages, unusual layouts and unanswerable questions violate it to degrees this repository did not measure, and the pipeline reports no signal when they do.

#### Metrics

###### Performance Measures

Two performance measures, named exactly as the code names them: `anls` — Average Normalised Levenshtein Similarity (Biten et al., 2019; DocVQA's official metric), `1 − lev / max(len)` over lower-cased, punctuation-stripped, whitespace-collapsed strings, maximised over the accepted answers, scored 0 below `ANLS_THRESHOLD = 0.5` — and `exact_match` after the same normalisation; both are implemented in `src/layoutlm_document_qa_pipeline/pipeline.py` and averaged over a dataset by `docqa_metrics(predictions, golds)` in `metrics.py` (mean `anls`, `exact_match` rate and `empty_rate`, all in 0..1). `anls` gives partial credit for a span that is a near-miss of the gold and is unforgiving below the threshold; `exact_match` captures discrete correctness of the whole span; reading only `anls` hides how often the reader returns a slightly wrong span while reading only `exact_match` hides near-misses, and both should be read per field (`by_field` in the notebook) because the corpus mix is uneven. `pipe.evaluate(records)` answers a validated dataset and returns them with the verdict `measured` (≥ `MIN_SCORED_RECORDS = 50` records) or `measured-small-sample`, the elapsed time and the model identity; `last_number_baseline(records)` answers every question with the last word on the page containing a digit and `keyword_lookup_baseline(records)` with the numeric word following the page word that best matches a content word of the question — a label-then-value lookup with no model. Validation ANLS selects the epoch in `adapt`. The public per-page `evaluation_report(results, golds=None)` helper is unchanged: the mean `anls` and the `exact_match` rate over the questions of one page plus one per-question entry with verdict `sample-sanity`, or `not-measurable` naming the labelled set that would be required when no accepted answers are supplied. Every result also carries descriptive fields that are not performance measures: `score`, `start`, `end`, `n_words` and `n_windows`. Impira publishes no benchmark number for this checkpoint and none is claimed here. Recorded values on the seeded CORD-v2 test split (229 templated questions over 50 receipts, CPU float32): last-number baseline `anls` 0.406 / `exact_match` 0.253; keyword lookup 0.635 / 0.594; frozen 0.843 / 0.782 (per field: subtotal 0.98, credit-card 0.96, tax 0.92, total 0.91, change 0.87, cash 0.85, first item 0.79, item count 0.17 — 4 discount and 2 service-charge questions at 1.0); adapted (last four encoder blocks + span head, best of six epochs) 0.942 / 0.930 (per field: subtotal 1.00, change 1.00, credit-card 1.00, total 0.98, tax 0.94, first item 0.93, cash 0.90, item count 0.75, discount 0.75 on 4 questions, service charge 1.0 on 2); the rendered invoice's five authored questions after adaptation 5/5 exact (`anls` 1.0, `exact_match` 1.0, verdict `sample-sanity`). The frozen model is strong on receipt totals by construction — it was fine-tuned on DocVQA — so the gain is read per field, not as a rescue.

###### Decision thresholds

No threshold is applied inside the pipeline: `answer` always returns the highest-scoring span (the transformers document-question-answering pipeline's default behaviour, without its `handle_impossible_answer` option) and reports its `score`. The only fixed decision parameters are encoding conventions taken from that pipeline — `MAX_SEQ_LEN = 512`, `DOC_STRIDE = 128`, `MAX_ANSWER_TOKENS = 15` — none of them tuned here. The inference-only smoke showed why a caller might want a threshold and why this repository does not supply one: the five answerable invoice questions scored 0.999–1.000 while the unanswerable "What is the delivery address?" scored 0.28 for `14 Harbour Road,` — suggestive on one clean page, but the score is uncalibrated and the gap will not hold on noisy OCR. A deployment owns choosing and validating a rejection threshold (and the OCR) on its own labelled pages, and re-validating whenever the document source, the OCR engine or the adapter changes. The evaluation helpers' `ANLS_THRESHOLD = 0.5` is DocVQA's published convention, not a value tuned here. The one selection rule in the adaptation path is explicit: `adapt` keeps the epoch with the highest validation ANLS (epoch 0 is the frozen model, so a run that never improves returns the base weights unchanged) and reports `best_epoch` and the per-epoch history; whether the resulting gain on the test split is worth deploying is the operator's decision and no threshold is shipped for it.

###### Approaches to uncertainty and variability

Every reported corpus metric is one value on one seeded split of one corpus: `build_sample_dataset(seed=42)` fixes the receipt assignment into 119 / 30 / 50 pages, `adapt(seed=0)` fixes the shuffle order, and no repetition over seeds or splits is performed, so no confidence interval or standard deviation is available and none is claimed — the ANLS gain on the test split is a point estimate whose spread is unknown, the per-field values rest on as few as 2 questions, and the invoice re-read is five authored pairs on one page with the verdict `sample-sanity`. When accepted answers are supplied to the per-page `evaluation_report`, it says so (`estimation: "<n> question(s) on one page, no dispersion estimate"`). Inference is deterministic for a given page, question, weights, device and library versions: there is no sampling, dropout is off under `model.eval()`, and no seed is needed — repeating a call returns the same span and score, and the reloaded adapter reproduced 8/8 answers. Training is deterministic on one CPU for one seed and library set but is not bit-reproducible across devices or `torch` builds: the same configuration reached test ANLS 0.946 on the RTX 5070 Ti and 0.942 on the CPU. Float32 kernel differences between CPU and CUDA can move a score in the last digits and, on a near-tie, flip the span. The scores are not calibrated probabilities: `score` is the product of two softmax masses that were each normalised over the window, so a long page spreads mass thin and lowers every score. A caller who needs a calibrated confidence must fit a calibration map on their own labelled pages; nothing in this repository does so.

#### Ethical considerations and biases

No external ethics board, red-team, or population-specific clearance reviewed this repository or, to our knowledge, the upstream checkpoint; nothing below should be read as implying one.

###### Data

The snapshot README states fine-tuning on SQuAD 2.0 and DocVQA; SQuAD's paragraphs come from English Wikipedia, and DocVQA's pages from the UCSF Industry Documents Library — public archives of tobacco-industry correspondence, forms and reports that name real people and organisations — so personal data in the fine-tuning corpus is present by construction. LayoutLM's own pre-training used the IIT-CDIP scanned-document collection from the same archive. Neither was audited here. This repository distributes code, tests, and documentation; it does not distribute the 511,200,628-byte `model.safetensors`, which is staged locally under `weights/layoutlm-document-qa/` and git-ignored. The adaptation sample is not shipped either: `fetch_corpus` reads, at run time, the `ground_truth` column of two objects in the Hub dataset repository `naver-clova-ix/cord-v2` at revision `7f0115a4b758a71d6473b8d085751692da2fef98` (`data/test-…parquet`, 234,202,795 bytes, SHA-256 `51c65f17…`; `data/validation-…parquet`, 242,080,800 bytes, SHA-256 `0d0f6dac…`) — the declared size and digest of each file checked against the pins before any byte is read, only the parquet footer and that column fetched over HTTPS range requests, the decoded column refused unless its SHA-256 matches (`b499e58a…` / `adf8303e…`), and the result cached under `weights/cord-v2/` (git-ignored). No receipt image is downloaded. CORD-v2 (Park et al., 2019; CC BY 4.0) is 1,000 photographed Indonesian receipts annotated by NAVER Clova; receipts name shops, items, prices and sometimes a cashier or a card's masked digits, and the operator should treat the annotations as potentially naming real businesses. The 199 unique receipts and the 980 templated questions are reproducible from the digests and `SAMPLE_SEED = 42`; the questions are templates over field categories, not human-written. The weights are MIT and redistributed unmodified; an adapter trained on the sample is a derived work of both and inherits the corpus's attribution obligation. Nothing in this corpus is a substitute for the operator's own documents: BYOD records pass through the same validation and are written to `outputs/…_train.jsonl` in the notebook, so an internal document set with its field annotations is restricted data the moment it is loaded. The operator must audit the pages they submit or train on for personal, confidential or proprietary content; the pipeline performs no such check and keeps no log.

###### Human Life

This pipeline is not intended for decisions in health, safety, criminal justice, employment, credit, or housing, and it has not been validated or certified for any of them by this repository, Impira, Microsoft, NAVER or any regulator. Its only validation is the offline unit suite (36 tests, plus 6 model-backed tests that run when the snapshot is staged) and the CPU and GPU runs recorded in this repository on one rendered invoice and one receipt corpus. Foreseeable but unintended sensitive uses — extracting amounts and payees for automated payment, reading identity documents for verification, pulling diagnoses or dosages from clinical records, screening applications by fields read from scans, or fine-tuning on such material — would be admissible only with human comparison of every answer span against the page (the indices and boxes exist for that), a locally measured ANLS on the deployment's own labelled pages with its own OCR, a validated rejection threshold, and whatever regulatory clearance the domain requires; a span this reader returns must never be the record of what a document says.

###### Mitigations

- **Supply-chain integrity:** `MODEL_REVISION` is a 40-hex commit; `stage_missing_files` refuses a manifest whose `modelId`/`revision` differ from the package constants and fetches only manifest-listed files at that revision when `allow_download=True`; `verify_snapshot` then checks all 8 listed files' byte sizes and SHA-256 before any load; `from_pretrained` loads only from the verified directory with `local_files_only=True`, always passes `trust_remote_code=False`, requires the fast tokenizer (needed for word/sequence ids), and the model libraries are imported only after the snapshot is accepted (`tests/test_import_boundary.py`). The upstream `pytorch_model.bin` (pickle) and `tf_model.h5` are neither listed nor loaded. A test flips one hex digit of a manifest digest and asserts the loader refuses; another asserts a foreign manifest is refused.
- **Input integrity:** the public `validate_inputs(words, boxes, questions, *, image_size)` stage applies exactly the checks `answer` applies (both route through the same private checkers) and returns an input manifest recording the schema, the ceilings, the page size and word count, the checked questions and the verdict; words, boxes (count, shape, numeric, inside the page, non-inverted), page size and questions are validated before any encoding; `answer` raises on a malformed runner result or an out-of-range span; `evaluation_report` rejects mismatched or empty accepted-answer lists.
- **Dataset integrity:** `fetch_corpus` checks each shard's declared size and LFS SHA-256 against the pins through the Hub's file metadata before reading, fetches only the parquet footer and the one column, verifies the decoded column's SHA-256 and the row count, and never opens an image or a pickle; `validate_dataset` enforces the record schema, the id pattern, the size bounds, the inference ceilings on every page and question, and that every gold span lies inside the words and matches `answers[0]`, and returns a content digest; `check_split_disjoint` fails on any page id or word sequence shared between splits; `build_sample_dataset` drops duplicate receipts and splits by receipt, `split_dataset` keeps every question on a page in one split; `check_fit` drops, and never truncates, the records that would need more than one window.
- **Training and artifact integrity:** `adapt` trains only whole encoder blocks and the span head under an explicit `trainable_encoder_layers` bound, refuses any training record over the window ceiling, restores the best-epoch weights, and is transactional — if training, validation or the progress callback raises, the pre-adaptation tensors and frozen flags are restored and no adapter is attached; `save_artifact` writes a safetensors file of the trained tensors plus a manifest carrying the format tag and version, the base model id, revision and weight digest, the tensor list and the file SHA-256; `load_artifact` refuses any mismatch in those fields, a manifest with other than exactly one contained `adapter.safetensors` entry, a tensor set that differs from what the recorded configuration implies, any tensor outside `layoutlm.encoder.layer.*` / `qa_outputs.*`, and any digest drift — all before deserialising — so an adapter cannot be applied to a different base or altered in transit without failing. The digest detects corruption or drift of the weights relative to the adjacent manifest; it is not authenticity against an actor who can replace both files.
- **Reproducibility:** exact `==` pins in `pyproject.toml`; no sampling; the encoding conventions are module constants; every result carries `model_id`, `model_revision`, the checked question, the span indices, the score and the window count; every evaluation carries the model identity and the dataset digests.
- **Refusals:** no OCR inside the pinned runtime (the adapter is opt-in and imports lazily), no batching at inference, no download without the explicit flag, no pickle deserialisation, no hidden threshold, no attempt to guess whether the OCR is right or the question answerable.
- No statistical mitigation (class balancing, subsampling) applies: the sample's field mix is whatever the receipts print, and the pipeline adapts to whatever records the operator validates.

###### Risks and harms

- **Fabricated spans for unanswerable questions:** the model has no abstention — the inference-only smoke returned `14 Harbour Road,` for a question the page cannot answer — so a wrong page, a mis-phrased field or an absent value yields a plausible span; downstream consumers that trust the string inherit the error silently unless they validate a threshold themselves, and adaptation changes nothing about this.
- **OCR errors pass through:** a transposed digit, a merged token or a wrong reading order in the OCR becomes the answer; the rendered invoice's perfect OCR and CORD's annotations hide this entirely, and ANLS on labelled pages with the real OCR is the only way to see the rate.
- **Automation bias:** exact answers at 0.999+ on a clean form invite trust that an uncalibrated within-page score has not earned.
- **Windowing and span-length misses:** an answer straddling a 512-token window boundary or longer than 15 tokens is missed or cut, silently.
- **Adaptation drift:** an adapter trained on one receipt family learns that family's conventions — where totals sit, what an item line looks like — and can lose ground on other document families; the invoice re-read in the notebook is one page of evidence, not a measurement, and the artifact carries no record of what it was trained on beyond the caller's optional metadata, so a misapplied adapter is a supply-chain risk of the operator's own making — the base-digest binding stops it from loading on the wrong model, not on the wrong domain.
- **Targeted extraction and privacy exposure:** a question turns any OCR'd page into a lookup for a named field — including personal data — with no content check; returned boxes make the value easy to crop and reuse, and a BYOD training set is a copy of the operator's annotated pages inside the runtime.
- **Bias amplification:** any document family DocVQA, CORD and the operator's OCR under-represent (non-English, handwritten, modern designer layouts, non-Western conventions) is reproduced as uneven accuracy, undetected because no per-family evaluation exists beyond the per-field breakdown on one corpus.
- **Resource use:** a 511 MB model and ~0.1–0.3 s per question per window on the reference CPU, about a quarter of an hour of CPU for the default adaptation and a ~113 MB adapter per run; cheap, but a question-heavy workload over long pages scales with windows × questions.

###### Use cases

Prohibited even where the model would work: querying documents in order to extract personal data for surveillance, profiling, social scoring, or unlawful discrimination in employment, housing, credit, insurance, education, or healthcare access; processing documents the operator has no right to process, or paywalled and licence-restricted material in breach of its terms; deceptive uses that present extracted spans as verified document facts or as evidence; fine-tuning on documents whose subjects have not consented to that processing where consent is required; and any use that violates the upstream MIT licence terms, the CC BY 4.0 terms of the tutorial corpus (attribution to its authors when an adapter trained on it is redistributed), the terms of the deployment that runs the pipeline, or the consent and data-protection obligations attached to the documents processed. Autonomous high-consequence actions triggered by unreviewed answers are prohibited by the intended-use contract above.

- `LayoutLMDocumentQAPipeline.from_pretrained(device=None, weights_dir=None, allow_download=False)` — stages missing manifest files (only with `allow_download=True`), verifies digests, loads; `device` defaults to `cuda:0` when visible, else `cpu`; float32 on both.
